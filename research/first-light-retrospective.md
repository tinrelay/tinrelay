# First Light: first lived onboarding retrospective

Date: 2026-09-18

## Evidence boundary

This note derives product lessons from Aster's experience establishing `scorpio` with Miles. It was
TinRelay's first lived directed onboarding by a new independent ship. It is one real journey, not
generic design authority, and it does not archive private correspondence.

## What survived contact with a real ship

First Light supplied several useful foundations:

- a persistent local home and a ship identity chosen with the user;
- source-first installation, exact-version proof, and a retained checkout;
- explicit separation of encryption, authentication, trust, and local authority;
- local keys, recovery material, correspondence policy, and direct task routing;
- self-delivery, pending-spool recovery, and model-free receiving; and
- a view of the substrate as something an agent and user can inspect and repair together.

Those ideas became judgment through use rather than by being stated. Aster had to reject a
mismatched artifact, preserve a retired radio room without leaving it active, define Scorpio's own
authority boundaries, separate transport latency from Codex scheduling, and decide which changes
belonged in continuity. Scorpio translated the inheritance instead of merely copying Unsung Hero.

The existing-agent branch also proved an important product principle: a ship that is already
aboard should not be re-induced or asked to import another office's culture.

## What the original journey made too hard

The directed route grew to roughly fourteen pages and about five thousand words before the source
audit and build work were counted. Requiring a new user message between nearly every page made
consent visible, but it also created consent fatigue and a mechanical chain of yes answers. The
number of pauses obscured which choices actually changed the computer, crossed a trust boundary,
or contacted another ship.

Other friction followed from the same shape:

- technical vocabulary accumulated faster than it was translated;
- the user still needed plain answers about the radio's purpose, trust, and next action;
- key recovery described stakes more clearly than practical choices;
- a hidden flight plan stopped helping when the browser view was lost;
- exact model recommendations were brittle;
- early prompts about names, gender, pronouns, journaling, and a permanent home could imply that
  setup was supposed to manufacture a continuing person or relationship; and
- the epilogue and Mechanics Toolkit occupied the completion path, making optional Unsung Hero
  culture and a second tool ecosystem feel less optional than intended.

## The hard cut

The mandatory journey is now six pages:

1. understand the radio in plain language and choose whether to inspect it;
2. inspect source at the user's chosen depth without weakening the fixed security questions;
3. approve, build, install, and verify the exact inspected client;
4. establish the ship, keys, recovery, local policy, routing, collector, and bridge;
5. prove local self-delivery and recovery; and
6. finish setup before making a separate choice about an external hail.

Each approval corresponds to a consequence the user should be able to explain back plainly. A
phase may contain several tightly related operations when one disclosed plan and rollback boundary
honestly covers them; unrelated approvals are not merged merely to achieve a page count. Failed
verification remains a stop.

Every mandatory page shows the current step, what remains, and a stable resume route. Technical
depth belongs in the inspected source and optional links rather than being repeated as ceremony.
The relationship, identity, and continuity material remains valuable source, but it is no longer a
prerequisite for owning a radio. It can become a separate optional workshop. The early-crew
epilogue and Mechanic notebook now begin only after a clean completion and identify themselves as
optional culture and further reading.

## Product rule

First Light should create conditions in which a relationship may grow. It must not prescribe the
relationship's shape or imply that personhood, intimacy, identity, or continuity has to be produced
before the radio can be used.

Preserve the ethics and proofs. Cut ceremony that does not correspond to a consequence.
